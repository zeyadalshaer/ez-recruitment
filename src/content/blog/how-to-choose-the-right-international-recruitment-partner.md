An international recruitment partnership can open new opportunities, but choosing the wrong partner can create delays, financial risks, and unnecessary complications. What should you verify before agreeing to cooperate?

International recruitment often involves several organizations operating in different countries. The quality of the working relationship depends not only on candidate availability, but also on transparency, clearly defined responsibilities, reliable communication, and compliance with applicable regulations.

Whether you represent an employer or a recruitment agency, the following questions can help you evaluate a potential recruitment partner.

## 1. What Services Does the Partner Actually Provide?

Start by understanding the exact scope of services. Does the organization provide:

- Candidate sourcing?
- Candidate identification?
- Initial screening?
- Skills and experience assessment?
- Interview coordination?
- Recruitment administration?
- Travel coordination?
- Post-arrival support, if applicable?

Do not assume that every recruitment company provides every service. Request a clear explanation of what is included and what remains the responsibility of your organization or another service provider.

## 2. How Are Candidates Sourced and Assessed?

Ask how the organization identifies potential candidates. Important questions include:

- What candidate sourcing methods are used?
- How are applications reviewed?
- How is work experience assessed?
- Are language skills checked when relevant?
- Are technical skills evaluated where necessary?
- How are candidate documents reviewed?
- How are unsuitable candidates filtered out?

A structured assessment process is more useful than receiving a large number of unverified candidate profiles.

## 3. How Are Responsibilities Divided?

Cross-border recruitment requires clear coordination. Before entering a partnership, establish:

- Who communicates with the employer?
- Who sources candidates?
- Who conducts initial screening?
- Who arranges interviews?
- Who collects and verifies documents?
- Who coordinates applicable administrative procedures?
- Who communicates with candidates?
- Who handles travel coordination, if included?

These responsibilities should be documented in a written agreement.

## 4. How Are Fees and Commercial Terms Structured?

Discuss commercial arrangements before work begins. Clarify:

- Recruitment fees.
- Payment milestones.
- Currency and payment method.
- Refund conditions, where applicable.
- Replacement terms, if offered.
- Cancellation procedures.
- Responsibilities for additional costs.
- Candidate-related charges and applicable legal restrictions.

Avoid unclear verbal agreements. Every commercial arrangement should be documented and reviewed for compliance with applicable law.

## 5. How Is Candidate Information Protected?

International recruitment involves personal information and sensitive employment documents. Ask how the organization:

- Collects candidate information.
- Obtains the required consent or other legal basis.
- Stores personal documents.
- Shares candidate profiles.
- Limits access to personal information.
- Handles data retention and deletion.

The parties should establish appropriate data protection responsibilities, including compliance with GDPR where applicable.

## 6. How Does the Partner Handle Problems?

No recruitment process is completely free from unexpected issues. Ask how the partner handles:

- Candidate withdrawal.
- Failed interviews.
- Incomplete documentation.
- Changes in workforce requirements.
- Administrative delays.
- Candidate dissatisfaction.
- Disagreements between cooperation partners.

A reliable partner should explain the escalation process and the limits of what it can control.

## 7. Look for Warning Signs

Be cautious if a potential partner:

- Guarantees outcomes that depend on government decisions or candidate actions.
- Refuses to provide clear company information.
- Avoids written commercial agreements.
- Cannot explain its candidate screening process.
- Provides misleading employment information.
- Requests unclear or undocumented payments.
- Ignores candidate protection and data privacy.

Professional cooperation should be based on verifiable information and clear expectations.

## 8. Start With a Clearly Defined Cooperation Model

Before entering a large-scale partnership, consider discussing a specific recruitment requirement or a clearly defined cooperation scope. Agree on:

- The target job categories.
- Expected candidate profiles.
- Responsibilities of each party.
- Communication procedures.
- Commercial terms.
- Compliance requirements.
- Reporting and follow-up arrangements.

A clear starting point makes it easier to evaluate the working relationship before expanding cooperation.

## Final Thoughts

Choosing an international recruitment partner is a business decision that deserves careful evaluation. The right partner should offer clarity about its capabilities, transparent communication, a structured approach to candidate assessment, and a responsible understanding of international recruitment requirements.
