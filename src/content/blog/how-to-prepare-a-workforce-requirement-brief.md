A recruitment partner cannot find the right people if the actual requirement is unclear. Before requesting 30 workers, 100 candidates, or an entire production team, what information should you provide?

A workforce requirement brief is the document that explains what an employer needs from a recruitment partner. It provides a common reference for candidate sourcing, screening, evaluation, and coordination.

A well-prepared brief reduces assumptions, improves communication, and helps recruitment partners focus on candidates who match the actual job.

## 1. Identify the Positions and Required Numbers

Start with a clear list of job titles and the number of vacancies for each position. For example:

| Position | Required Number |
| --- | --- |
| Production Workers | 20 |
| Packaging Workers | 10 |
| Machine Operators | 5 |

Avoid using broad descriptions such as "general workers" when different responsibilities require different candidate profiles. If recruitment will happen in several stages, specify the expected number for each stage.

## 2. Describe the Daily Responsibilities

Explain what candidates will actually do. For a production worker, responsibilities may include:

- Working on a production line.
- Handling products and materials.
- Following production instructions.
- Maintaining workplace cleanliness.
- Following safety procedures.
- Supporting the production team.

Descriptions should reflect the real workplace rather than using attractive but inaccurate language.

## 3. Define Skills and Experience

Divide the requirements into mandatory and preferred criteria. Consider:

- Previous work experience.
- Technical skills.
- Physical requirements relevant to the job.
- Language requirements.
- Educational background, if necessary.
- Relevant certificates or licenses.
- Ability to work shifts.

Do not request qualifications that are not genuinely necessary for the position.

## 4. Explain the Working Conditions

Candidates need an accurate understanding of the job. Include:

- Workplace location.
- Working days.
- Daily working hours.
- Shift arrangements.
- Overtime arrangements.
- Salary and payment frequency.
- Accommodation availability.
- Transportation arrangements.
- Meals or other employment benefits, if provided.
- Safety equipment and workplace conditions.

If some conditions are not yet finalized, identify them as pending rather than making assumptions.

## 5. Define the Recruitment Timeline

Specify:

- Preferred recruitment start date.
- Expected interview period.
- Desired employment start date.
- Whether positions are urgent.
- Whether candidates can start in several groups.
- Any operational deadline that affects the recruitment process.

A realistic timeline allows recruitment partners to assess feasibility before making commitments.

## 6. Clarify Employment and Administrative Arrangements

Explain the intended employment structure. Relevant questions include:

- Which legal entity will employ the workers?
- Where will the work take place?
- What type of employment arrangement is intended?
- Who will coordinate the required work authorization procedures?
- Who will handle travel and arrival arrangements?
- Which party is responsible for onboarding?

These details must be reviewed according to the applicable laws and the specific recruitment model.

## 7. Use a Standardized Workforce Requirement Template

A practical brief can follow this structure:

### Workforce Requirement Brief

**Company Information**

- Company Name:
- Industry:
- Workplace Location:
- Main Contact Person:
- Business Email:

**Vacancy Information**

- Job Title:
- Number of Workers:
- Job Description:
- Required Experience:
- Essential Skills:
- Preferred Qualifications:

**Employment Conditions**

- Salary:
- Working Hours:
- Shift Pattern:
- Weekly Days Off:
- Accommodation:
- Transportation:
- Other Benefits:

**Recruitment Planning**

- Preferred Starting Date:
- Interview Requirements:
- Expected Recruitment Timeline:
- Can Workers Start in Groups?
- Is International Recruitment Acceptable?

**Administrative Information**

- Intended Employing Entity:
- Work Location:
- Responsible Party for Work Authorization:
- Additional Notes:

## 8. Keep the Brief Updated

Workforce requirements can change. Production volumes may increase, project timelines may move, and the number of vacancies may be revised.

Whenever a change occurs, communicate it to the recruitment partner as soon as possible. An updated brief helps maintain consistency between the employer's expectations and the candidate profiles being presented.

## Final Thoughts

A clear workforce requirement brief is one of the most practical tools in international recruitment. It helps employers communicate their needs, gives recruitment agencies a defined search target, and provides a common reference throughout the process.
